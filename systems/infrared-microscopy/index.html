Infrared laser-scanning microscopy

Infrared light, scanning optics, and instrument control for chemical imaging.

Molecules absorb infrared light at characteristic wavelengths. We can use those differences to make images with chemical contrast, without adding stains or fluorescent labels. In the Chemical Imaging and Structures Laboratory (Bhargava Lab) at UIUC, we develop laser-scanning microscopes for these measurements.

The figures here show one instrument and an example image from our team’s 2023 publication.

From light to an image

A tunable infrared laser selects the wavelength used for a measurement. Scanning mirrors move the focused beam across the specimen, while a detector records the signal. Matching those readings to positions builds an image.

Repeating the scan at other wavelengths adds chemical information at each location. Selected absorption bands can become image channels; measurements across wavelengths form a spectrum.

Reading the chemical contrast

In this section of an adult zebrafish, false color combines selected infrared absorption bands. The anatomical labels help connect the chemical patterns to recognizable structures.

Controlling the measurement

An acquisition brings together laser settings, scanner motion, detector readings, and data storage. The software connects the requested measurement to those individual operations and handles the resulting data.

My software work includes ScopeEngine, which organizes acquisition workflows, and ciMicroscope, a desktop application for operating the microscope. The software page goes into the acquisition framework, device interfaces, and movement of data between components.

Building and operating the instrument

These microscopes are team projects. My main work is control software and firmware, with additional contributions to electrical design. I also help build, align, integrate, and maintain the instruments. That work connects the software to the physical setup: optical alignment, electrical interfaces, and acquisition control all need to work together for a measurement.
