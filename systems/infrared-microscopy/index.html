Infrared laser-scanning microscopy

Microscope control software and firmware, electrical design, and hands-on instrument work in the Bhargava Lab at UIUC.

Infrared microscopy uses molecular absorption to form chemical images without adding stains or fluorescent labels. A laser-scanning microscope records these measurements across a specimen, combining spatial information with infrared spectral contrast.

These instruments are developed in the Chemical Imaging and Structures Laboratory (Bhargava Lab) at UIUC.

The instrument brings together a tunable infrared laser, scanning optics, positioning hardware, detectors, and synchronized data acquisition. Optical alignment, electrical interfaces, firmware, and control software all contribute to making those measurements possible.

My work

My main work is control software and firmware. I also contribute to electrical design and to building, aligning, integrating, and maintaining the instruments.

Acquisition software

The control software includes ScopeEngine, an acquisition framework, and ciMicroscope, a desktop application for infrared laser-scanning microscopy. It coordinates device control, acquisition workflows, and processing of the acquired data.

The ScopeEngine / ciMicroscope project page describes the software architecture, including the separation of acquisition workflows from vendor drivers and the transfer of acquired data through shared memory.

The published instrument

The 2023 Nature Communications paper describes the team’s infrared spectroscopic laser-scanning confocal microscope for whole-slide chemical imaging. Its instrument layout and characterization show the published optical configuration, spatial-resolution measurements, and example imaging results.
